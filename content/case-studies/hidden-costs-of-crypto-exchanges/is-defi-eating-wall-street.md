In the late hours of some cold Manhattan morning, someone on the 42nd floor of a Wall Street office is struggling to move capital across borders. It’s 2025. Why are we still relying on outdated pipes to move money? Why does it take three days to settle trades in a world where memes go viral in three seconds?

Meanwhile, halfway across the world, a pseudonymous address just provided liquidity to a pool on Uniswap, earned fees, and moved their yield to another protocol — all in under 10 minutes. No emails. No clearance. No banker approval.

_Welcome to decentralized finance._

# The TradFi Execution Problem

![A crowd of people waiting under a clock beside a diagram of a cross-border payment hopping from bank to bank through SWIFT and correspondent institutions](/assets/hidden-costs/p1-tradfi-execution.png)

Traditional finance (TradFi) institutions run on a web of legacy systems — fragmented, opaque, and riddled with middlemen. Trade settlements, custodian layers, clearing houses, off-chain agreements, reconciliation cycles… every step introduces time, friction, and cost.

Sure, the infrastructure was revolutionary when it started. But today, it’s just sluggish. The execution isn’t just slow — it’s misaligned with the pace of modern markets. And more importantly, it’s exclusive. You need credentials, capital, and connections to even get a seat at the table.

# DeFi: Open Execution for an Open World

![A banker hands cash into a ring of crypto tokens, Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB and Tezos, and it comes out the other side to a second banker](/assets/hidden-costs/p1-open-execution.png)

In DeFi, the rules are code, and the gatekeepers are none _(I mean, there are gatekeepers for security, but no middlemen ; ))_. Anyone with a wallet and an internet connection can trade, lend, borrow, provide liquidity, or build financial products on top of open protocols.

This isn’t an idea on the fringe anymore. The numbers speak for themselves:

![DefiLlama dashboard showing 113.109 billion USD total value locked across DeFi, with the TVL curve, DEX volume, fees and stablecoin market cap](/assets/hidden-costs/p1-numbers-1.png)

![DEX dashboard showing 16.61 billion USD in 24-hour volume and 116 billion USD in seven-day volume, with market share by DEX and by chain](/assets/hidden-costs/p1-numbers-2.png)

- **$113B+** in Total Value Locked (TVL) across DeFi protocols (as of May 2025).
- Tens of millions of wallets have interacted with smart contracts on Ethereum, Solana, and other chains.
- Protocols like **Aave**, **Curve**, **MakerDAO**, and **Uniswap** now serve roles that mirror (and in some ways outperform) traditional banks, money markets, and exchanges.

But this isn’t just about money moving in. It’s about _how_ it moves. The operating model of DeFi is fundamentally different. And that’s the edge.

# Code Over Committee

In TradFi, trade execution is an afterthought; in DeFi, it’s the heart of the product. Execution logic is written into smart contracts — automated, trustless, and auditable. Capital can be deployed programmatically. Liquidity can be incentivized transparently. Market access is algorithmic.

This enables an entirely new class of financial interaction:

- **24/7 markets**: No open/close bells.
- **Global accessibility**: No KYC wall blocking retail in Argentina from participating in markets in the U.S.
- **Composable products**: Protocols can be stacked like Lego blocks.

Of course, this doesn’t come without risks. But the _design space_ is bigger. And when the design space is bigger, innovation flows faster.

# The Money Flow Is Real

_Let’s talk capital._

![Illustrated banker in crypto-coin glasses with a fan of dollar bills rising behind his head](/assets/hidden-costs/p1-money-flow.png)

Institutions and sophisticated investors are no longer just watching from the sidelines. They’re entering the ecosystem — directly or via proxies — to capture yield, hedge risk, or participate in governance. From JPMorgan’s experiments with on-chain assets to BlackRock dipping into tokenized treasuries, the line between TradFi and DeFi is getting blurrier by the day.

But it’s not just about TradFi adapting. It’s about a new ecosystem growing with different rules.

And nowhere is this transformation more visible than in **exchanges** — the place where markets are made.
