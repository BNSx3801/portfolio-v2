# But, Why Are We Talking About Exchanges?

At the heart of every financial system — whether it’s a centuries-old marketplace or a modern stock market — there’s always been one constant: the exchange.

![From barter, to an old woman holding gold coins, to a hooded Pepe trading crypto at a computer](/assets/hidden-costs/p2-exchanges.webp)

It started with simple barter systems, where people traded grain for cattle or salt for fabric. Fast forward a few thousand years, and now we’re watching tickers on the _NASDAQ, LSE,_ or _BSE,_ tracking billions of dollars flowing through global markets every day. Exchanges have always been where markets come alive — where value is priced, traded, and discovered.

Traditional exchanges have done a lot right. They’ve handled massive transaction volumes, connected capital across continents, and shaped the financial world we know today. But let’s be honest — they’ve also come with baggage.

![Three frogs labelled Sluggish Settlement, Middlemen Involvement and Lack of Transparency](/assets/hidden-costs/p2-baggage.webp)

Lack of transparency, sluggish settlement times, layers of middlemen, and barriers to access have made these systems more exclusive than efficient. The machinery works, but only if you’re inside the club.

_And that’s where things started to shift._

When Decentralized Finance — DeFi — entered the scene, it didn’t just propose new financial tools. It posed a question: _What if we could rebuild the entire exchange system — without the gatekeepers?_

At the center of that rethink were two types of platforms: **Centralized Exchanges (CEXs)** and **Decentralized Exchanges (DEXs)**. They both promised access to crypto markets, but how they delivered on that promise couldn’t be more different.

Let’s break them down — one at a time.

# The Big Decision: CEXs or DEXs?

_**Another Day, Another Article, Another Story.**_

![A bearded giant hands a boy in round glasses a small pouch of gold coins](/assets/hidden-costs/p2-houses.webp)

Let’s say your name is _Harry Potter,_ and you’ve just arrived at _Hogwarts._ _Hagrid_ has given you some magical coins — and they’re powerful. They can be traded, swapped, or even multiplied — but only if you choose the right house.

Just like in the movie, you’re to be sorted into houses. But here, we only have two: _Gryffindor_ (Decentralized) and _Slytherin_ (Centralized). _(The reputation of the house precedes!)._

Though unlike the movie, you have the privilege of choosing your house.

If you choose to take a step toward _Slytherin_ (the Centralized House), the path is clean, paved, and well-lit. There are tall, gleaming towers whispering things like _“Fast Trades Here!”_ and _“24/7 Customer Support!”_ Then, a helpful — but shady-looking — wizard in a green suit greets you at the gate.

_“This way is safe,”_ he says. _“Give us your magical coins — we’ll keep them for you. You won’t have to worry about a thing.”_

Now listen to me — the moment someone says _trust me,_ that’s the moment I trust them the least.

Back to the story. For a while, it works. Your coins are easy to trade. You know what’s happening with them, how they’re multiplying and all. You can even trade large amounts without shaking the system. But soon, you start noticing fine print carved into the walls.

![A goblin banker leaning over a vast pile of gold coins outside the bank's columns](/assets/hidden-costs/p2-fine-print.webp)

Turns out, you don’t actually own your tokens anymore. They’re sitting in a _Goblin Vault_ you can’t access directly. And while goblins are trustworthy — well, that’s in the story, right? What if the tower decides to lock the door? Or worse, it gets attacked — and you’re left outside, empty-handed.

Stressful, isn’t it? Maybe you should’ve chosen the other house.

# A New Approach? Perhaps a Decentralized One!

In December 2021 alone, centralized exchanges lost **$196 million** to hacks and exploits. In 2022, that number ballooned to over **$735 million**. And in 2023, even after all the headlines, they still bled close to **$600 million**.

![News headlines: 1.7 billion USD stolen from crypto investors, 3.9 billion lost in 2022, 477 million vanishing from bankrupt FTX, and BitMart compensating victims of a 196 million hack](/assets/hidden-costs/p2-hacks.webp)

After every breach or mishap, almost every CEX echoed the same line:

> **“Your funds are safe with us.”**

But in a space built on self-custody and transparency, that kind of reassurance stopped carrying weight.

The response wasn’t just skepticism — it was a shift in direction. Enter **Decentralized Exchanges (DEXs)** — platforms designed to remove trust from the equation entirely.

![Popeye flexing under the word DEXs, surrounded by: no intermediaries, smart contract based, no custodians, rewards everywhere, self-custody, no KYC requirements, no censorship risk](/assets/hidden-costs/p2-dex.webp)

_No intermediaries. No custodians. No centralized choke points. Just smart contracts and self-custody._

With DEXs, users retain full control of their assets. They connect their wallets, initiate a swap, and the protocol executes the trade — all on-chain, all transparent. It’s not about trusting a platform anymore. It’s about trusting the code.

So, there are a few key players in a decentralized exchange — the liquidity providers, the users, and the protocol itself. We’ll get into all of them in the next article. But before we do, let’s take a quick step back and ask:
